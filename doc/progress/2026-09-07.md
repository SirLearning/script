## 2026-09-07 — SUPP — IBS=1 meaning and cross-subgenome overlap

- IBS=1 is not whole-genome identity. PLINK=1 means every jointly called site in that matrix matched (hets included); missing sites and sites not in the panel are ignored. Hom=1 only requires zero mismatch among both-homozygous SNPs.
- Within each subgenome, every PLINK=1 pair is also hom=1 (`plink_only=0`). Hom adds extra pairs (8–12 on `test_common_inter` A/B; 552 on D).
- Counts (off-diagonal, IBS written as 1.000000):

| mod | sg | PLINK=1 pairs | hom=1 pairs |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| test_common_inter | A | 37 | 45 |
| test_common_inter | B | 34 | 46 |
| test_common_inter | D | 328 | 880 |
| test_thin | A | 225 | 360 |
| test_thin | B | 147 | 195 |
| test_thin | D | 1360 | 2704 |

- Among pairs whose two samples exist in A, B, and D (7138 shared IDs): `test_common_inter` PLINK=1 is ABD-consistent for 9 / 360 union pairs; hom=1 for 12 / 915. D contributes most of the extras (305 PLINK D-only, 843 hom D-only). A∩B is 16 PLINK pairs and 19 hom pairs.
- The three KING kinship-0.5 dups (`CRR877168`–`170`, `567`–`568`, `667`–`669`) are hom=1 on all three subgenomes on `test_common_inter`, but are not PLINK=1 on all three (PLINK often 0.999999, not 1.000000).

## 2026-09-07 — KING negatives vs low wheat heterozygosity

- Question: is KING still untrustworthy because wheat het is too low (many negative kinship values), which was why IBS was used instead?
- Observed het on published `*.sample.het.rate.info.tsv`: `test_thin` A/B/D median 0.33% / 0.48% / 0.34% (~98% of samples < 1%). `test_common_inter` A/B/D median 0.85% / 0.68% / 1.60%. Same inbred regime as before.
- Identity KING tables (`--king-table-filter 0.177`) hide the bulk: A/B keep 143–618 pairs, of which 3 sit at kinship ~0.50 with IBS0=0 (the known dups). D keeps far more (1376 / 7057), so the 0.177 first-degree cutoff is not calibrated here.
- Diagnostic (not a numbered NF run): unfiltered `plink2 --make-king-table` on a random 400-sample slice of `test_thin` A (`12stats.genome/_scratch_king_neg`). 79800 pairs; 99.996% kinship < 0; median −4.51; 15 pairs −inf (HETHET=0); only 3 pairs ≥ 0 (max 0.089). Matches KING-robust `(HETHET − 2·IBS0) / (Hi+Hj)` with HETHET median 0.00040 vs IBS0 median 0.011.
- Conclusion: the negative-kinship problem is unchanged for using KING as a relatedness continuum. It does not invalidate the kinship≈0.50 duplicate hits. IBS remains the usable genome-wide similarity; KING remains a high-pass duplicate screen, not a replacement for IBS distances.

## 2026-09-07 — Why not IBD (PLINK PI_HAT) for dedup

- IBD was already in `sample_identity_compare.nf` (`plink --genome` after LD prune, cap 25k SNPs, `--min 0.25`). It is not unused; it is unusable as a duplicate screen in this panel.
- `test_thin` A: 55119 pairs at PI_HAT≥0.25; median PI_HAT=1.0; 73% have Z2=1 and 84% have Z1=0. Even PI_HAT=1 is 40367 pairs that form **one cluster of all 7675 samples** (CS_/SAMEA involved in 41409 pairs). Metadata recall is 2014/2014 only because the whole panel is merged.
- `test_common_inter` A is better but still wrong-scale: PI_HAT≥0.9 → 7438 pairs, 26 clusters, drop 4338, only 93/5076 metadata pairs, 881/2014 metadata-removed recovered. Human duplicate threshold 0.9 does not mean identity here.
- Cause: PLINK method-of-moments IBD assumes HWE/outbred sharing. Wheat het is ~0.3–1%, so sharing is dumped into Z2 (unrelated inbreds look like MZ twins). CS clones then chain the graph. KING 0.50 / IBS still recover the three true IBS=1 dups; IBD=1 does not isolate them.
