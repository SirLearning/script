## 2026-10-28 — Does site density matter for the IBS values? (split-half estimate)

- The literal request was to re-derive one chromosome per subgenome at thin rate 0.03 instead of 0.002473. The raw per-chromosome pfiles are 57 GB each (chr001 holds 97.3M variants), so 3% = ~2.9M sites per chromosome and a multi-hour pass just to thin; the question was answered instead by splitting the existing filtered sites in half (`39run_site_sampling_noise`).
- Half-vs-half |ΔIBS| on 878 combine pairs (A/B/D averaged): median 0.00004, p90 0.00039, p99 0.00079, max 0.0038. Implied standard error at the current density ≈ **0.00012** (95% band ±0.00023), falling to 0.000034 at 12x density and 0.000018 at 44x.
- Practical effect on the cutoffs: only **2 pairs** sit within the current ±0.00023 band of 0.995 and 30 of 0.999; at 44x density those shrink to 0 and 10. So denser sites would not move any conclusion — the IBS differences that decide pass/fail are 1-2 orders of magnitude larger than the sampling noise.

## 2026-10-28 — Selfing-model het filter (corrects an HWE mistake) and the published comparison

- Earlier in the day the het filter was implemented with `plink2 --hardy` (HWE). That was wrong for wheat: the pipeline's own filter is a **selfing model**, `p_excess = P(H ≥ h_obs | MAC, F)` under `Binomial(MAC, (1-F)(1-MAC/2N))` (`genetics/genomics/variant/mac_het_excess.py`), and it keeps `MAC > 0 & p_excess ≥ 1e-6`. HWE assumes random mating, so it rejected 65-82% of sites; the selfing model rejects 21-27%.
- Median inbreeding per subgenome on test_common_inter: **A 0.860, B 0.836, D 0.719**. Sites kept by the het step: A 541,564/690,490 (78.4%), B 1,102,619/1,454,150 (75.8%), D 175,123/239,339 (73.2%); intersected with PopDep keep80: 63.8% / 60.7% / 64.9%.
- `35run_selfing_filtered_ibs`: hom-SNP IBS recomputed for 878 combine pairs and 2000 random AABBDD pairs on both site sets. Median IBS (mean of A/B/D), full → filtered: combine 0.99864 → 0.99933, random 0.97412 → 0.97014. The gap widens from 0.0245 to 0.0292 — the filtered-out sites were making *random* pairs look more similar, so filtering improves discrimination instead of collapsing it (the HWE version had pushed every combine pair above 0.997 because it kept only ultra-conserved sites).
- Above 0.999 the combine set grows from 363 to 467 pairs while random pairs stay at 0; at 0.995 both are ~550 vs 0 random.
- `38run_publish_selfing_hist_fix2`: published the two comparison figures — `all.sample.combine_ibs.vs_random.all_sites.hist.png` and `…vs_random.filtered.hist.png` — via the new `subworkflows/tmp/publish_ibs_hist.nf`. ## 2026-10-28 — Pricing the hom-SNP IBS cutoff in sites

- `31run_hom_ibs_site_counts`: decoded the PLINK beds directly (the published pair tables carry `n_hom = 0`, so their counts are unusable) to measure how many sites the metric is built from. Median both-homozygous sites per pair on test_common_inter: **A 574,818 / B 1,204,920 / D 208,798**, i.e. ~2.0M genome-wide. A pair scoring hom_ibs 0.995 therefore differs at ~0.5% of those sites = **~10,000 homozygous-homozygous mismatches** (2,874 + 6,025 + 1,044); 0.99 costs ~20,000 and 0.999 ~2,000. The closest pairs in the data (ABD_1090 / CS_sg_2017_60X) differ at 11-28 sites out of 0.24-1.4M.
- Consequence for threshold choice: 0.995 is roughly 300x looser than the tightest pairs, so it is a defensible "same population, some heterogeneity" level rather than a "same plant" level. The ratio alone also overstates confidence when few sites are scored, so pair it with a minimum `n_both_homozygous` filter (`--hom_snp_min_nobs`, currently 0).
- Side finding: hom_ibs in the combine table is the mean of A/B/D ratios, so the D-only bed count can disagree with the stored value (ABD_0305/ABD_0321: table 0.8308, D-only 0.543) — the disagreement is the A and B subgenomes being much closer.
